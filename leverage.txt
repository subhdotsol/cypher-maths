LEVERAGE & ORDER TYPES IN PREDICTION MARKETS — CYPHER RESEARCH
================================================================


PART 1: LEVERAGE
=================


HOW INTO/SPACE DOES LEVERAGE
-----------------------------

They use a CLOB (Central Limit Order Book) + Share Tokens model.
Completely different architecture from Cypher's parimutuel pools.

Their model:
  1. Shares are actual SPL tokens — YES token, NO token
  2. Deposit $1 USDC → mint 1 YES share + 1 NO share
  3. Shares trade on an order book at variable prices ($0.15, $0.65, etc.)
  4. If your side wins → each share pays out $1.00
  5. If your side loses → each share pays out $0.00

Because shares have a FIXED PRICE at the time you buy them, leverage works:

  You want to buy at $0.15 per share.
  Without leverage: pay $150 → get 1,000 shares
  With 5x leverage: pay $30 margin → control 1,000 shares ($150 position)

  If price goes to $0.30:
    Position value = 1,000 × $0.30 = $300
    Your equity = $300 - $120 debt = $180
    Profit = $150 on $30 margin = 500% ROE

  If price drops to ~$0.13:
    Position value = 1,000 × $0.13 = $130
    Your equity = $130 - $120 = $10  (below 10% maintenance margin)
    → LIQUIDATED

Their key parameters:
  - Initial Margin (IM): 20% required to open position
  - Maintenance Margin (MM): 10% to keep position open
  - Max Leverage: 10x
  - Liquidation formula: (Entry Price × Shares - Margin) / (Shares × (1 - MM))
  - Staged liquidation: 25% tranches, not all at once, 10% insurance fund penalty

The key: shares have a MARK-TO-MARKET PRICE at every moment.
The system knows what your position is worth right now,
so it can calculate when to liquidate you.


CYPHER'S MODEL: PARIMUTUEL POOLS
---------------------------------

  1. No shares. No tokens. Just pool ratios.
  2. You deposit money into a pool (Yes/No/outcome).
  3. Your payout is calculated ONCE at the END based on final pool sizes.
  4. There's no "current price" of your position during the market.
  5. There's nothing to liquidate — your money is already in the pool.


WHY LEVERAGE DOESN'T WORK WITH PARIMUTUEL
-------------------------------------------

Leverage needs three things parimutuel doesn't have:

1. A REAL-TIME POSITION VALUE
   Order book: your 1,000 shares at any moment = shares × current_price.
   Parimutuel: your $10 in the Yes pool is just $10 in the pool.
   There's no price to mark it against until settlement.

2. SOMETHING TO LIQUIDATE
   Leverage = you borrowed money. If position goes against you, protocol
   force-closes and takes back what you borrowed. Order book sells your
   shares on the market. Parimutuel has no one to sell to — money is
   locked in pool until settlement.

3. CONTINUOUS EXIT CAPABILITY
   Order book traders can sell shares at any time.
   Parimutuel bettors can't withdraw — pool is locked.
   If you can't exit, you can't be liquidated.


POSSIBLE PATHS FOR CYPHER TO ADD LEVERAGE
------------------------------------------

PATH A: Build a CLOB Market Type
  Completely different architecture. Add a new market type to Cypher
  alongside parimutuel (YesNo/Multi) and Accuracy.

  Would need:
    - YES/NO share tokens (SPL tokens, mint/burn mechanism)
    - Deposit $1 USDC → mint 1 YES + 1 NO token
    - On-chain order book for trading shares
    - Margin engine tracking positions, collateral, liquidation thresholds
    - Liquidation bots
    - Insurance fund for bad debt

  This is not an upgrade to parimutuel — it's a separate product.
  But Cypher's enum-based state design already supports adding new
  market types. Would be a 4th MarketType variant.

  Pros:
    - Real leverage with real liquidation
    - Continuous trading (exit anytime)
    - Real price discovery
    - What the "big" markets (Polymarket, Into/Space) do

  Cons:
    - Massively more complex (order book matching engine on-chain)
    - Needs market makers / liquidity providers
    - Liquidation infrastructure
    - Basically building a mini exchange


PATH B: Synthetic Leverage via Lending Wrapper
  Keep parimutuel underneath, build a lending layer on top.

  Flow:
    User has $30, wants 5x leverage on Yes.
    1. User deposits $30 as collateral into a Leverage Vault
    2. Leverage Vault lends them $120 (from a lending pool funded by LPs)
    3. $150 total goes into the parimutuel Yes pool as a normal bet
    4. If Yes wins:
         payout calculated normally from pool
         user repays $120 + interest to vault
         keeps the rest as profit
    5. If No wins:
         user's $30 collateral is forfeit to the lending pool
         lending pool absorbs the $120 loss (or insurer does)

  Problem: Who provides the $120?
    - Lenders take on risk that the bet loses
    - Need high interest rates or insurance
    - No liquidation during the market — lender is locked until settlement
    - Very different risk profile from Into/Space's real-time liquidation
    - Lender could lose 100% of principal if the bet loses

  Would need:
    - Lending pool account (LPs deposit capital for others to borrow)
    - Leverage Vault PDA per leveraged bet
    - Interest rate model
    - Post-settlement repayment logic
    - Insurance fund for default scenarios


PATH C: Pseudo-Leverage with Multiplied Payouts
  Simplest approach. Not real leverage — amplified risk/reward.

  Flow:
    User bets $30 with 5x multiplier selected.
    Protocol treats it as if user bet $150.
    If user wins: payout calculated on $150 position, profit amplified.
    If user loses: user only loses $30 (their actual deposit).

    The difference ($120 of "phantom" pool contribution) comes from
    a house/insurance fund that subsidizes the leverage.

  Example:
    Market: yes_pool = 500, no_pool = 500 (before this bet)
    User bets $30 on Yes with 5x
    Protocol adds $150 to yes_pool (user paid $30, house covers $120)

    If Yes wins:
      share = 150 / 650 = 23.08%
      winnings = 0.2308 × 500 = $115.38
      total_return = 150 + 115.38 = $265.38
      house takes back $120
      user gets $145.38
      user profit = $145.38 - $30 = +$115.38

    If No wins:
      user loses $30
      house loses $120 (from insurance fund)

  Problem:
    - Cypher becomes "the house" — takes directional risk
    - Needs a large insurance/reserve fund
    - Fund can be drained by coordinated winners
    - Not truly decentralized


LEVERAGE CONCLUSION
--------------------

Leverage and parimutuel are fundamentally incompatible.
Into/Space can do leverage because they have:
  ✓ Shares with real-time prices
  ✓ Order book for continuous trading
  ✓ Exit liquidity at any moment
  ✓ Mark-to-market for liquidation calculations

Cypher's parimutuel pools have none of these.

Realistic options ranked by feasibility:
  1. Path A (CLOB) — best product, hardest to build
  2. Path B (Lending) — works but lenders bear heavy risk
  3. Path C (Pseudo) — easiest but Cypher becomes the house

If leverage is a priority, Path A as a new MarketType is the
clean long-term answer. The state.rs enum design already supports
adding MarketType::Leveraged with its own MarketData variant.

PART 2: MARKET ORDERS VS LIMIT ORDERS
=======================================


HOW INTO/SPACE DOES IT
-----------------------

The foundation: shares as SPL tokens.

  Deposit $1 USDC → mint 1 YES token + 1 NO token (SPL tokens on Solana)

  YES token: worth $1 if outcome happens, $0 if not
  NO token:  worth $1 if outcome doesn't happen, $0 if it does

  YES price + NO price ≈ $1.00 always (enforced by arbitrage)
  If YES=$0.65 and NO=$0.40 (total $1.05):
    → Arbitrageur mints pair for $1, sells both for $1.05, pockets $0.05
    → This pushes prices back to summing to $1.00

These tokens live in wallets. They can be transferred, listed on
order books, traded freely — just like any SPL token.


LIMIT ORDER
  You set the price. The order sits on the book waiting to be matched.

  Alice: "I want to BUY 100 YES shares at $0.35"
    → Order sits on the book. Nothing happens yet.
    → Alice is a MAKER (adds liquidity). Pays 0% fee.

  Later, Bob: "I want to SELL 80 YES shares at $0.35"
    → Matches against Alice's order.
    → Bob is a TAKER (removes liquidity). Pays dynamic fee.
    → 80 shares transfer from Bob → Alice at $0.35 each.
    → Alice's order still has 20 shares remaining on the book.


MARKET ORDER
  You accept whatever the best available price is. Fills instantly.

  Order book state:
    SELL side (asks):
      100 YES @ $0.37  (oldest)
      200 YES @ $0.38
      50  YES @ $0.40

  Carol: "I want to BUY 150 YES shares at MARKET price"
    → Takes 100 @ $0.37 = $37.00
    → Takes 50  @ $0.38 = $19.00
    → Total: 150 shares for $56.00 (avg price $0.3733)
    → Carol is a TAKER. Pays dynamic fee.

  Market orders eat through the book from best price to worst.
  That's why big market orders get worse average prices — SLIPPAGE.


PRICE-TIME PRIORITY
  Two orders at the same price?
    → The one placed FIRST gets filled first.

  Two orders at different prices?
    → The one with the BETTER price gets filled first.
      (lower ask or higher bid)


THEIR FEE STRUCTURE
  Makers (limit orders): 0% fee — incentivized to add liquidity
  Takers (market orders): dynamic fee based on market probability

  Buy fee formula:
    F_buy(p)  = 0.02% + (2.00% - 0.02%) × (1 - (p/100)^1.3)

  Sell fee formula (Gaussian, peaks at 50% probability):
    F_sell(p) = 0.02% + (1.00% - 0.02%) × e^(-0.5 × ((p-50)/σ)^2)

  Examples:
    At 20% probability → buy fee ~1.76%, sell fee ~0.12%
    At 80% probability → buy fee ~0.10%, sell fee higher

  Early/contrarian bets cost more in fees.
  Consensus bets cost less.

  Full trade example:
    Open:  1,000 YES shares at $0.30 (30% prob) via market order
           Cost = $300 + 1.6% fee ($4.80) = $304.80

    Close: sell at $0.80 (80% prob)
           Proceeds = $800 - 0.04% fee ($0.32) = $799.68

    Profit: $494.88 (162% ROI)


WHY CYPHER CAN'T DO THIS (RIGHT NOW)
--------------------------------------

Parimutuel has no order book, no shares, no continuous trading:

  CLOB (Into/Space)                   PARIMUTUEL (Cypher)
  ──────────────────                  ─────────────────────
  Tradeable share tokens              No tokens — just pool deposits
  Buy/sell at any time                Deposit once, locked until settlement
  Price set by traders                Price is just pool ratio
  Limit orders sit on book            No book — money goes straight to pool
  Market orders fill instantly        No orders — just place_bet()
  Can exit before settlement          Cannot exit
  Maker/taker distinction             Everyone is the same

The core issue: in parimutuel, when you "bet $10 on Yes," that $10
is IMMEDIATELY mixed into the pool. There's no share token you hold,
nothing to sell back, no order book to post on.


WHAT CYPHER WOULD NEED TO ADD CLOB
------------------------------------

1. SHARE TOKEN INFRASTRUCTURE

  New instruction: mint_shares(market, amount)
    → User deposits $1 USDC
    → Program mints 1 YES token + 1 NO token to user's wallet
    → Uses SPL Token program, one mint per outcome per market

  New instruction: burn_shares(market)
    → User returns 1 YES + 1 NO token
    → Program burns both, returns $1 USDC
    → Arbitrage mechanism that keeps prices honest

2. ORDER BOOK ACCOUNTS

  New account: OrderBook PDA per market
    → bids: sorted list of buy orders (price descending)
    → asks: sorted list of sell orders (price ascending)

  New account: Order PDA per order
    → owner, side (bid/ask), price, quantity, timestamp, filled, status

3. TWO INSTRUCTION TYPES

  place_limit_order(market, side, price, quantity)
    → Posts order on book. Waits for match. Maker.

  place_market_order(market, side, quantity)
    → Matches against best available orders immediately. Taker.

  cancel_order(order)
    → Removes unfilled limit order from book. Returns tokens.

4. MATCHING ENGINE

  On every new order:
    → Walk the opposite side of the book
    → Match at best price first, then next best, etc.
    → Partial fills allowed (100 share order can match against 60 + 40)
    → Unmatched remainder stays on book (limit) or fails (market)


HOW HARD IS THIS?
------------------

Building an on-chain CLOB is one of the hardest things in Solana.
It's what Phoenix, OpenBook, and Into/Space do — each with massive
engineering effort.

The matching engine alone is complex:
  - Sorting orders by price-time priority
  - Partial fills (order matched across multiple counterparties)
  - Cancellations (removing orders, freeing capital)
  - Self-trade prevention
  - Handling token transfers atomically with matches

PRACTICAL PATH FOR CYPHER:
  - Keep parimutuel (YesNo, Multi, Accuracy) as the core
    → simpler, fairer, works, no liquidity bootstrapping needed
  - If CLOB is wanted later, add as MarketType::Clob
    → own MarketData::Clob variant in state.rs
  - OR integrate with an existing CLOB (Phoenix / OpenBook)
    rather than building a matching engine from scratch
    → Cypher mints share tokens, lists them on Phoenix
    → Phoenix handles all order matching
    → Cypher handles settlement (burn shares, pay $1 to winners)

PART 3: COMPARISON SUMMARY
============================

                        PARIMUTUEL (Cypher now)    CLOB (Into/Space)
                        ──────────────────────     ─────────────────
Tradeable shares?       No                         Yes (SPL tokens)
Order book?             No                         Yes
Limit orders?           No                         Yes
Market orders?          No                         Yes
Leverage?               No                         Yes (up to 10x)
Exit before settle?     No                         Yes
Price discovery         Pool ratio (passive)       Trader orders (active)
Liquidity needed?       No (pools self-fill)       Yes (makers required)
Complexity              Low                        Very high
Fairness                High (no early advantage)  Depends on liquidity
MEV risk                Low                        High (front-running)
Best for                Simple prediction bets     Active trading markets
